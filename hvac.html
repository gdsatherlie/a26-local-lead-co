Try these on the call

Throw her the calls your office actually gets.

"My furnace is blowing cold air."

Mention a baby or an elderly parent at home. She gets name, address and number, flags it urgent, and offers to connect you to the on-call tech.

"I smell gas."

She tells you to get out and call 911 from outside, then takes your details. No guessing, no do-it-yourself advice.

"What's a tune-up cost?"

She quotes the prices you give her, and only those. She never diagnoses, never quotes a repair, never promises an arrival time.

"Can I talk to a real person?"

She takes your name and number first, then connects you live to the owner's cell with a quick heads-up only the owner hears.

"Do you do Carmel?"

She knows the service area, hours, financing and maintenance plan. Outside the area, she says so politely and still takes the details.

Hang up after "hello."

Watch your phone. Callers who hang up before leaving details get an instant text back, so the job isn't lost.
